# Bank Marketing Campaign Effectiveness & Customer Conversion Analytics

### An End-to-End Data Analytics Project for Improving Marketing Campaign Conversion

## Business Problem

A bank runs direct marketing (phone) campaigns to encourage customers to
subscribe to a term deposit. This project analyzes historical campaign data to
answer:

> **Which customer and campaign characteristics are associated with successful
> term-deposit subscriptions, and how can the bank improve marketing campaign
> conversion?**

## Dataset

- **Name:** Bank Marketing Dataset (bank-additional-full.csv)
- **Source:** UCI Machine Learning Repository — https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/222/bank+marketing
- **Original research:** S. Moro, P. Cortez and P. Rita, *"A Data-Driven Approach
  to Predict the Success of Bank Telemarketing"*, Decision Support Systems,
  Elsevier, 62:22-31, June 2014.
- **Size:** 41,188 records, 20 input attributes, 1 binary target (`y`)
- **Target:** whether the customer subscribed to a term deposit (`yes` / `no`)

The dataset is unmodified in `data/raw/`. It was **not** created by this project
— all credit for data collection belongs to the original authors and UCI.

## Key Findings

- Overall conversion rate is **≈11.3%**.
- **Students (≈31%)** and **retirees (≈25%)** convert far above average;
  blue-collar and services workers convert below average.
- **Cellular contact (≈14.7%)** converts nearly 3× better than telephone (≈5.2%).
- Conversion is strongly **seasonal** — March, September, October and December
  outperform the heavily-used month of May by a wide margin.
- Customers with a **successful previous campaign** convert at **≈65%**, versus
  ≈9% for customers never previously contacted.
- More repeated contact attempts within a campaign are associated with **lower**,
  not higher, conversion (diminishing returns).

## Predictive Model

A **Logistic Regression** model was built to estimate the probability a customer
will subscribe, using customer, campaign, and macroeconomic features (the
`duration` field was deliberately excluded to avoid data leakage, since it is
only known after a call is completed).

| Metric | Score |
|---|---|
| Accuracy | 0.83 |
| Precision | 0.36 |
| Recall | 0.65 |
| F1-score | 0.46 |
| ROC AUC | 0.80 |

The model is intended as a simple, interpretable tool for **ranking customers by
likelihood to convert** — not a black-box replacement for judgment.

## Business Recommendations

1. Prioritize cellular contact over telephone.
2. Re-engage previously successful customers first.
3. Cap contact attempts at 2–3 per customer per campaign.
4. Sequence call lists by the model's predicted conversion probability.
5. Concentrate campaign volume in higher-converting months.
6. Monitor macroeconomic indicators as a leading signal for campaign scale.

Full detail is in `report/Project_Report.pdf` and the notebook.

## Limitations

- Data covers one Portuguese bank's campaigns (2008–2010); findings may not
  generalize to other markets or periods.
- `duration` was excluded from the predictive model to avoid leakage, so the
  model reflects what's knowable **before** a call, not during one.
- Precision is modest due to class imbalance (~11% positive class); the model
  favors recall, appropriate for a "who should we call next" use case.
